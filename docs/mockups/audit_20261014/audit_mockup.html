Mock-up of the audit Dexter runs on every ship visit, rebuilt on the HR console's crew, ship and contract data. Two phases: before the visit, the seafarer is asked for the machine documents and the console reads them; on the ship, Dexter audits from his phone, one step per screen. Crew placements and console facts shown here are examples; the history table holds Dexter's real rows from the Forms export.

Mobile-first rules the build has to keep

What the screens above assume, written down so the build is held to it.

Works without signal

Ship Wi-Fi drops. The draft is saved on the phone at every tap (IndexedDB), photos queue, and the audit can be filed offline; it syncs when the phone sees the server. The top strip says which state the phone is in. This is the one requirement that changes the architecture: an installable PWA with a service worker, not a plain page.

Photos are the evidence

A full-width Take photo button on every step that takes evidence (machines, inventory, tools, uniform). Photos are shrunk on the phone before upload (longest side 1600px, about 300 KB) so a visit does not push 40 MB over a ship link. Each photo is tagged with its step and machine automatically.

One tap when nothing is wrong

Every checklist step starts in its clean state: All present, Pass, Accurate, Nothing missing. A clean ship is seven taps plus three ratings and three short sentences. Typing is only ever about what is wrong.

Thumb sizes

Every control is at least 44px tall: rows 52px, chips 40px, rating squares 54px, the bottom buttons 52px. Text inputs are 16px so iOS does not zoom. Back is top-left, the next action is in the bottom bar, always the same place.

One step per screen

Seven steps, a progress line, the step name in the header. Back and Next never lose anything. A step with a required comment or a required answer says so under the field and holds Next, with the reason, instead of failing at the end.

The phone is Dexter's, the data is the console's

Nothing is typed that the console already knows: ship, crew, contract, serials, parts names, the last visit, the crew's own count and the life percentages from the Management Lists. The laptop view is the same single column, 560px wide, centred; nothing else to design.

The audit is gated

No request, no audit. The Start button exists only once every machine has its Pattern 43 and Management List or a "machine down" reason from the crew, the count is in, and the pre-visit report has gone out. Resend and extend are Dexter's; "start anyway" is nobody's. Miguel, 14 Oct 2026: "without that completion, I don't want him to start the audit."

The seafarer's page is the HON upload, reused

Same single-use signed link as the sign-off and shipboard-review pages, no login. Each file is checked as it lands (size above zero, 12 pages, four channels for Pattern 43) and a bad scan is asked for again on the spot; scans are shrunk client-side to 2–5 MB because a 64 MB scan does not cross a satellite link (Brain, recp1N5c3hEqQ7wTW). Every upload saves on its own; the link keeps working until the deadline.
