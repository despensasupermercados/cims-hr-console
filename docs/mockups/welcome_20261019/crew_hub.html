Inventory and supply chain

Placing your orders

Check the inventory team's ordering schedule first, so your order catches a container with time for review, approval and delivery.

  1. 1Get the order schedule from inventory management onboard, every week.
  2. 2Review last month's consumption and today's counts onboard.
  3. 3Build your statistics file with everything you gathered.
  4. 4Create the order on OBP by the schedule's due date. US ships keep the next 3 months on OBP, international ships the next 6.
  5. 5One order a month is best. A spare container? Split the order to balance stock.
  6. 6Tell shoreside management about any request, change or approval.
  7. 7DG3 shoreside approves. Then the order is created in CrunchTime, approved by the GSM / DCM and the IM (MOT).
  8. 8DG3 receives the PO two weeks before delivery to Weston; the order goes into a container and comes onboard.
  9. 9Inspect and accept the whole delivery on OBP within 24 hours. Report any shortage with backup data, per the SOP.
  1. 1DG3 sends the ordering schedule every two weeks or monthly. Review it.
  2. 2DG3 orders toner and paper for the Konica C4070.
  3. 3You create the orders for Konica C4070 parts. MFD toners and parts are sent as the ship needs them.
  4. 4Tell shoreside management about any request, change or approval.
  5. 5DG3 approves your order, gets the PO and processes it. It goes to the Blue Water Shipping warehouse, then onboard.
  6. 6Inspect and accept the whole delivery on OBP within 24 hours. Report any shortage with backup data, per the SOP.

Every day, every ship

  • Update OBP every day with any change in toner, paper and parts.
  • Keep your printer logs up to date. They are your backup data.
  • Check your usage once a week, so you never need an emergency order.
  • Can't avoid an emergency order? Raise it early, with the documents.

Ergonomics

Lift and work without getting hurt

Shoulders, back and knees take the strain onboard. The goal: nobody gets hurt.

Report it early. Pain, swelling, stiffness, numbness, weak muscles or tiredness that does not go away.
Four rules for every lift
  1. 1

    Keep the load close to your body.

  2. 2

    Keep it in front of you, in line with your spine.

  3. 3

    Don't twist. Turn your feet instead.

  4. 4

    Feet apart, back straight, head up. Let your legs lift.

The hit list: spot it, report it
  • Elbows out
  • Shoulder too high
  • Butts up
  • Twist and shout
  • High force
  • Contact
Butts up is the most common. If you see more backs than faces, the work is too low. Keep work above your knees, about 61 cm (24 in).
